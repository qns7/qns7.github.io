INSIGHTS & OUTLOOK

Navigating federal selection procedures, side projects, and new horizons.

Since March 2026, I’ve applied to three German federal agencies. I took an online assessment for one, spent much of the summer on a shortlist for another, and traveled to Berlin for an interview with the third. All three procedures ended by mid-September without a job offer.

A look back at the selection processes

Federal Criminal Police Office (BKA). The online psychodiagnostic assessment felt a lot like an IQ test. I went in without any preparation and, honestly, found it easy. I passed. I’d been told that passing meant I’d go to Mainz for an assessment center. About a week later, I got a rejection instead. I still find that weird.

Federal Office for Information Security (BSI). I applied through the “Digital Talent” initiative. Over the summer, the team sent me several personal updates confirming I was still on the shortlist. While I waited, I submitted my final university results, including a 1.0 for my master’s thesis and an overall grade of 1.9. By the final review, none of the open positions matched both my specialization and the kind of work I was looking for.

Federal Intelligence Service (BND). After an initial screening, I was invited to Berlin for a structured interview and an on-site assessment. I felt good about the discussions and tasks, the details of which are confidential. I estimated there were about 20 candidates that day for what appeared to be one opening. A week later, I received a rejection.

What sticks with me is how tedious the whole thing was. Between the three applications, I put more than half a year into this. Each agency sets the steps you have to follow, and from the applicant’s side you get very little insight into how they make their decisions. After what happened with Mainz, even knowing the next step on paper didn’t tell me much about what would actually happen.

I got some experience out of it, and I’m not bitter about the outcome. What bothers me is how much time and effort that machinery asks of you. You do your part, you wait months to find out where you stand, and when the last rejection lands, those months are gone.

Open to new opportunities

Since mid-September, I’ve broadened my search. I’m looking for work where I can think through a technical problem and get my hands on the implementation. I’ve also been exploring the Federal Volunteer Service (Bundesfreiwilligendienst, or BFD) and part-time or temporary positions while I look for a longer-term role. I’m also thinking about becoming a teacher.

Banks, insurance companies, and firms that develop weapons are still a no-go for me. I’ve yet to come across one where I’d feel good about putting my name to the work. That matters to me when I’m deciding where to apply.

What fills my days

Mathematics and music are my two loves. Whatever else I’m working on, I always come back to them. Between applications, I keep studying computer science and system architecture, alongside my interests in the natural sciences and project management.

Working on guitars and custom electronics gives me a different kind of problem to solve. Sometimes I’m tracing a fault in a circuit or adjusting a guitar’s setup. Other times the guitar works just fine and I’m curious enough to try a different wiring scheme and hear what changes.

I’m also building a software application from the ground up and steadily getting it ready for market. If you know of an opening, want to compare notes, or have a project we could work on together, I’d like to hear from you.
